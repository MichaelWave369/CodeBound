# Field-guide editorial standard

Every proposed guide must answer:
1. **Why:** immediate objective and the need it solves.
2. **Before:** prerequisites, people, equipment, materials, skills, environmental limits.
3. **Build:** numbered actions understandable without a network or brand-specific service.
4. **Verify:** checks that can fail and trigger correction.
5. **Risks:** what not to do, escalation criteria, and impact on nearby systems.
6. **Evidence:** public source URL, named publisher, publication/review date if available, reviewer, local context.
7. **Lifecycle:** storage, maintenance, repair, end-of-life, and community ownership.

Distinguish measurements from estimates, and link primary public-health or standards sources for high-consequence instructions. Do not call a guide validated merely because an AI generated it. Seek independent field or specialist review for water treatment, sanitation systems, medicine, construction, electricity, and food safety.

**Contribution boundaries:** no offensive weapons, explosives, coercive control, surveillance of vulnerable people, unsafe medical procedures, hazardous chemical synthesis, or unqualified high-voltage instructions. Preserve human rights, privacy, accessibility, and nonviolent cooperation. Use respectful, non-alarmist language.

**Review statuses**
- reference-backed: the main safety facts link to credible primary guidance, but this is not formal field certification;
- community-draft: a proposed method that needs deeper expert review before critical use.

The project should be useful even if society never collapses. Build community capacity now.
